# Workspace layout

Open **Layout** in the header (in **Menu** on narrow windows or at large UI scales) to choose a preset, show or hide panels, or adjust widths. These choices are saved on this device, separately from `.squid` files. They never add production history, move performers or change print coordinates.

- **Write** shows the Set list and Inspector at their default widths.
- **Review** keeps the Set list and expands the field by hiding the Inspector.
- **Rehearse** tucks both panels away and keeps the field controls and playback timeline.
- **Reset layout** restores Write and default widths. Other personal settings remain as chosen.

The active preset is pressed: it has an accent border, a selected background and a check mark, so the state does not depend on color alone. Resizing, collapsing or restoring a panel makes the layout **Custom**. No preset is then pressed, and the popover says *Custom layout: choose a preset to restore its panels and widths.* The **Layout** button's tooltip names the current layout (Write, Review, Rehearse or Custom).

Cast opens in place of the Inspector using its own saved width. Opening Cast from the header (or with **Dock Cast** or the Command palette) reveals it even if it was collapsed previously, leaves **Focus field** mode, closes any other panel open over the field, and moves keyboard focus to its search once Cast is docked or open over the field. If you had collapsed Cast, its overlay offers **Collapse**, as after **Show Cast**. On a small window or at a large UI scale Cast docks beside a narrower field; where even that does not fit, it opens over the field (see below). The full Cast table remains available from its panel. Returning to Design restores the Inspector's own layout choice.

Drag the divider beside a panel to resize it. A divider is also a keyboard stop: Left/Right resize in the direction of the arrow, Shift increases the increment from 10 to 40 pixels, Home/End use the width limits, and Enter collapses the panel. Escape cancels a pointer resize. The Layout sliders offer an alternative using the keyboard with a one-pixel increment and a readable pixel value.

Set list widths range from 160–360 px, Inspector from 235–420 px, and Cast from 280–480 px. Panels have visible collapse controls. A collapsed panel leaves a **Show** button beside the field. Layout changes that would hide an active input or position preview wait until it is finished or cancelled; restoring a hidden panel remains available.

## Small windows and UI scale

Layout responds to the *effective* window size: the window size divided by the UI scale. A 1366 × 768 window at 150% therefore lays out like a 911 × 512 window. The thresholds live in one module, `src/editor/breakpoints.ts`, and the editor exposes the result once as `data-header`, `data-transport` and `data-height` on `.studio`.

- **Header.** The header always stays one row. Below 1200 effective pixels, **Layout**, **Focus field**, **Help** and **History** move to the top of **Menu**, in that order, and Menu shows only its icon. Below 800 pixels, the Design/Cast/Sheet tabs and Save also become icon-only, with the same names in their tooltips. Command palette, Undo, Redo, Save, Export and workspace switching stay visible. Choosing **Workspace layout…** from Menu opens the same options and moves focus into them; Escape returns focus to Menu.
- **Panels.** The field keeps at least a 520 px container, enough for a 480 px drawn field at 100%. When space is short, open panels first shrink toward their minimum widths, then the set list tucks away, then the Inspector or Cast. A tucked panel keeps its **Show** button, which opens it over the field; one panel is open over the field at a time. Escape, clicking the surrounding field or the overlay's **Close** button (where a docked panel shows **Collapse**) closes it and returns focus to **Show** (after a click, without the keyboard focus outline and its help). A panel you collapsed yourself is different: **Show** restores it (the layout becomes Custom), and its overlay offers **Collapse**, which collapses it again. A panel you open stays open, docked where it fits and otherwise over the field, until you close it, switch workspace, side panel or **Focus field**, or the window grows enough to dock it beside a full-width field (the 544-pixel reserve below); after that a narrower window tucks it again. Tucking is temporary: it never changes the saved layout or preset, and panels dock again at their saved widths when the window grows.
- **Panels you ask for.** A side panel you explicitly open docks beside a narrower field before it tucks: the main workspace then keeps 360 effective pixels for the field instead of 544 (`DEMANDED_FIELD_RESERVE` in `breakpoints.ts`, the reserve used before UI-scale breakpoints). This applies to Cast opened from the header tab, **Dock Cast** or the palette, and to the Inspector while a transform, formation, spacing or coordinate preview needs its controls. The set list still tucks first. Below that width (about 676 effective pixels for Cast and 631 for the Inspector), and where docking would crowd the workspace (see the trade-offs below), Cast opens over the field with focus in its search, and a preview opens the Inspector over the field without dimming it: the field fits beside the overlay, so the preview, its banner, Apply/Cancel and playback stay visible. The same happens when the window shrinks during a preview.
- **Previews and panels over the field.** While a preview runs, its Inspector takes the place of any other panel open over the field, so Apply and Cancel are always reachable; meanwhile **Show Set list** is unavailable. If focus was in the panel it replaced, focus moves into the Inspector. This placement belongs to the preview and ends with it, whether you apply it, cancel it, press Escape or start playback: an Inspector that docked or opened over the field only for the preview tucks again, with no scrim, at every window size. (Before UI-scale breakpoints, an Inspector a preview opened over the field stayed open behind a scrim afterwards.) Only a panel you opened yourself stays open afterwards, over the field where it does not dock: an Inspector you opened with **Show Inspector**, also when the preview docked it, or a Set list you opened, which returns over the field. Focus left on no control, in a panel that is now hidden (such as the preview's Apply or Cancel) or under the dimmed field moves to that panel's heading, or otherwise to the field: its focused performer, or the field itself. A preview also restores an Inspector you collapsed in the saved layout (the layout becomes Custom), as it did before UI-scale breakpoints; otherwise none of this changes the saved layout.
- **Trade-offs of panels you ask for.** The transport follows the timeline's actual width. Beside an Inspector that docks only for a preview, the timeline is 360–544 effective pixels wide, so between about 631 and 791 effective pixels rehearsal passages and audio move into **More playback options** until the preview ends: below 520 px the transport has no room for them on its row. On short windows at large UI scales, such as 1014 × 650 or 1200 × 600 at 150%, docking beside the narrower field wraps the field toolbar and makes the workspace scroll. Docking is *crowded* when the workspace then scrolls more than 1 pixel further than it does without the panel; a window that already scrolls without the panel, like 1200 × 600 at 150%, counts only the added scrolling. A crowded Cast opens over the field instead, as on narrower windows, and a crowded preview opens the Inspector over the field beside it. The editor checks this after each update and whenever the workspace content changes size, for example when the 3D view's taller field floor appears beside a docked Cast. Cast you showed from its tab, **Dock Cast** or the palette, or Cast that holds focus, opens over the field when it becomes crowded, keeping focus in its search, rather than tucking away; Cast restored at startup on a crowded window starts tucked. The decision holds for the window size, panel, 2D/3D view and workspace it was measured in, and is dropped once the workspace without the panel grows enough that docking no longer adds scrolling; returning from 3D to 2D, for example, docks Cast again. This keeps a docked panel from adding scrolling and stops it from docking and tucking again as a classic scrollbar comes and goes.
- **Transport.** Play, set navigation (first, previous, next and last set), count stepping and the show count always stay on one row. When the timeline is narrower than 800 px, loop, metronome and playback speed move into **More playback options** (⋯). Below 520 px, rehearsal passages and audio move there too. The menu keeps their original order, closes when focus leaves it and returns focus to ⋯ on Escape. A dialog opened from the menu, such as **Rehearsal passages**, keeps it open underneath, so closing the dialog returns focus to the control that opened it. If a resize, a UI scale change or a docking panel moves a focused control, or the control that opened a dialog, into or out of the menu, focus follows it to its new place, or to ⋯ when it is folded away. Focus that has left the control stays where it is: after a click on plain text leaves focus on the page, a later resize, dialog or preview never moves it into the transport, so Space still plays and pauses.
- **Short windows.** Below 620 effective pixels of height, chrome spacing tightens so the field keeps its share. The field container never drops below 150 px. Below the smallest supported size (an 800 × 600 window at 150%), the workspace scrolls and the timeline stays pinned at the bottom so playback remains visible.

The desktop window can shrink to 800 × 600 native pixels; OS window decorations reduce its content height.

**Focus field** (Ctrl/⌘ + Shift + F outside text entry) hides the header, panels and field toolbar, leaving the field and timeline. **Exit focus** stays visible over the field; Escape also restores the workspace without clearing the selection. Panels never open over the field in focus mode; showing Cast beside the field, for example from the Command palette, leaves focus mode first. Focus is temporary and starts off after restarting the app. It does not choose a different 2D/3D view or change the written show.

Layout records added to older personal preferences use safe defaults and preserve the older Compact panel widths. Invalid layout fields recover independently, and oversized/out-of-range dimensions are bounded. Resetting all Preferences also resets layout choices.

## Validation and design review

Shared design guidance sections 1–5 apply. This change reuses the compact header, contextual Inspector, Cast, Lucide icons, shared help/focus controls, typed commands and native personal-preference codec. The new `workspace` desktop suite owns resizing, presets, focus, narrow/enlarged layouts and restart persistence; other suites retain field interaction, file editing and detailed Cast coverage. Use `pnpm test:workflows --suite=workspace` after building.

The [review evidence](evidence/workspace-layout/README.md) records before/after dimensions and screenshots. Other themes remain owned by #43; actual writer, assistive-technology and physical multi-display validation remain tracked by #64/#3. Automated window resize/zoom checks establish renderer behavior, without claiming physical device qualification.
