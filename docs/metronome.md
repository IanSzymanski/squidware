# Meter-aware metronome

The timeline's Metronome button toggles clicks; the same command appears in the Command palette. Open **Production settings → Metronome and count-in…**, or search **Metronome and count-in** in the palette, to configure accents, lead-in and rehearsal speed. Apply accepts the draft; Cancel or Escape discards it. Invalid grouping disables Apply and explains the error.

The optional production `meter` stores a numerator of 1–32, denominator 2/4/8/16 and positive accent groups adding to the numerator. Presets include 3/4, 4/4, 6/8, 9/8 and 12/8; Custom meter supports others, including 7/8 with 2+2+3. Missing meter, including old projects, uses 4/4 and `[4]` without modifying the project. One drill count is one denominator beat, and existing tempos remain counts/minute. A 6/8 move at 120 therefore has six eighth-note clicks per bar at 120 counts/minute, not 120 dotted-quarter beats/minute. Bar, secondary group and ordinary beats use distinct 1320/990/660 Hz clicks. Bar phase begins at show count zero and continues across set boundaries. This is a single production meter; measure maps, pickups and within-show meter changes remain #51.

Meter edits save/reopen through the canonical v2 codec, native files and recovery, and use one named undo transaction. Older v2 applications discard the optional property on save. Meter edits do not change counts, written tempos, coordinates or print geometry. Click enablement, zero/one/two count-in bars and 0.5×/0.75×/1×/1.25×/1.5× rehearsal speed are session state. Applying only session options does not dirty the project.

Count-in uses the tempo at the requested starting count and the selected rehearsal speed. Each Play, including resume, requests its configured lead-in; both the field and attached recording wait at the chosen count. Lead-in accents begin at the bar and repeat the configured groups. The first show beat follows the show's global meter phase. Fractional starts retain their exact count and resume clicks on the next whole count. Loops repeat the show or active rehearsal range without another lead-in. Changing speed/range/timing during a lead-in cancels the old queue; a speed change restarts that lead-in at the new rate. Pause, seek, dialog opening, replacement and unmount cancel queued clicks and their active tails.

The production AudioWorklet owns a 100 ms rolling click queue, replenished every audio render block. It synthesizes short decaying clicks at AudioContext sample times, independently of animation frames and UI timers. The plan contains only the move tempo spans, bounds, meter, speed and clock anchor, rather than all show beats. A 25 ms control timer samples the existing media-authoritative clock and synchronizes handovers/drift; it does not fire clicks. Click-only show time uses the same AudioContext clock. Media stalls pause future clicks when observed; media offsets, silent lead/tail and failed-recording fallback retain the existing clock behavior. Real drift over 50 ms replaces the pending queue; ordinary frame/timer quantization does not.

During a recording's count-in, the audio processor schedules only lead-in clicks. Show clicks begin when media confirms its actual start. A one-time cue retains the requested initial whole count even when startup confirmation or its control message arrives late; later beats keep their actual media-time anchor. A renderer blocked across that handover can delay the recording's start, but cannot skip ahead in the recording or emit ahead-of-recording show clicks. Click-only lead-in and ongoing clicks continue on the audio thread across UI stalls. Audio output/processor failures report **Click unavailable** in the transport and leave rehearsal controls usable.

Source tests verify event times at all five speeds, tempo boundaries, compound/custom accents, fractional ranges, loop edges, maximum-size tempo plans, canonical validation/save/reopen/undo and count-in cancellation/handover. Tests also execute the actual audio processor and inspect its PCM samples for onset times, accent frequencies, amplitude and cancellation. The `metronome` desktop owner exercises the production worklet with 1600 ms renderer stalls, count-in with and without a generated recording, blocked lead-in handover, native controls, invalid drafts, Escape, loops, themes, enlarged layout and unavailable-processor fallback. Existing `audio-clock`, `playback` and `rehearsal` owners retain media/geometry/subscription coverage. These are software checks, with no claim of speaker latency, listening sessions or physical-device qualification; the maintained validation policy does not require those gates.
