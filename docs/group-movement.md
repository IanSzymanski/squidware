# Group route movement

Pause at an arrival set in 2D and select at least two performers. Open **Group route movement…** in Formation tools or Commands. Choose a saved shared route, including a route drawn or placed through **Shared routes…**. Routes are production-local geometries; assigning a group is a separate reviewed edit. Each incoming move can contain up to 32 disjoint groups, with 2–500 ordered members per group.

**Members and leader** makes member 1 the leader/reference member. Choose a member and Move earlier/later to change rank, or explicitly check/uncheck performers. Performer identities stay attached to their existing source and destination dots. One performer cannot belong to two groups on the same move. Live formation geometry, membership and assignment restrictions are retained.

## Three motion modes

- **Follow the leader** evaluates the same leader distance program at `clock − rank × delay`. Delay is in counts, not distance. The first direction extends before its first cue; negative cue clocks explicitly describe earlier turns/stops when followers need that history.
- **Counter-march** uses the same delayed-follower rule, with explicit forward and reverse direction cues required. Every member reaches the same turn point and reverses there at its delayed count. A turn changes travel direction immediately at a continuous position; it does not model an arcing turn, acceleration or performer facing.
- **Conveyor** adds `offsetDirection × rank × interval` to the leader's distance. Interval is measured in yards along the route, using its bounded arc-length lookup rather than raw cubic parameter. Direction/stop cues affect all members together; signed member offsets and their cyclic order stay fixed through turns and loop seams.

**Initial route phase** is the leader/reference distance at route clock zero. **Route speed** is yards per count while moving. Cue directions are forward (+), reverse (−) or stop (0), with strictly increasing clocks. The first cue is at or before zero; following history can extend as far back as `−(members − 1) × delay`. Cue clocks are relative to the route window, not show count. Follow/counter-march member r receives a cue at local move count `entryCounts + cueClock + r × delay`; conveyor members receive it together at `entryCounts + cueClock`. A stop holds until another cue or the route window ends. Show tempo, pauses and playback speed determine when a count occurs; they never change its position.

On an **open route**, choose either rejection of insufficient history/length or explicit endpoint holds. Holds clamp out-of-range distances to the corresponding endpoint, so physical intervals may compress or coincide. Open conveyor capacity requires `(members − 1) × interval ≤ routeLength`. This is a storage/authoring constraint, not clearance certification.

On a **closed route**, positive and negative distances wrap at the saved seam, including multiple laps. Capacity requires `members × interval ≤ routeLength`: the last-to-first gap is `routeLength − (members − 1) × interval`, which must be at least the chosen interval. The control reports that gap; **Space evenly around the whole loop** sets `interval = routeLength / members`. Unequal remaining seam gaps remain fixed during motion. Corner seams retain their corners; tangent alignment does not imply speed continuity. Self-crossings remain visible and are analyzed as physical motion.

## Explicit entry, exit and individual paths

The route window is `[entryCounts, move.counts − exitCounts]` and must have positive duration. Positive entry/exit counts create straight submoves between the unchanged set dots and the corresponding route positions. Both connectors appear in the inspected path and are included in travel/stride/spacing checks. A zero connector requires the saved dot to match the route position within 10⁻⁸ yard; mismatches reject rather than snapping dots onto the route. Group motion and connectors use linear counts even when the surrounding move is eased. Existing move counts, tempo, easing and declared marching directions are retained.

Saved individual paths remain in the move as dormant travel. Applying a group over any such path requires **Allow group travel to override saved individual paths while grouped**. Removing a member or removing the group restores that member's saved individual travel; counts may have been explicitly rescaled in the meantime. Review removal through the same preview/checks before Apply. Group editing never silently bakes or discards the original path.

Individual path editing, drawing, interval copy/paste and formation-transition replacement are blocked for grouped members. Edit Group route movement or remove the member there first. Geometry changes/deletion of a referenced shared route are blocked until groups are removed or rerouted; name-only changes are allowed. Removing a performer from Cast requires removing its group memberships first. Ordinary endpoint edits remain subject to connector compatibility and existing live-formation rules.

## Preview, Apply and operations

**Movement preview** scrubs arbitrary fractional counts through the same sampler used by playback, 3D, Problems, neighbor paths and exports. The inspected member's path includes entry/exit; its dots are count ticks. The dashed route is reusable geometry. Section colors identify performers, a ring identifies the leader/reference member, and original outlines compare saved movement. Display toggles and theme/scale changes do not edit geometry.

Cards compare selected total/longest travel and conservative peak-demand bounds, plus full-cast spacing/crossing and stride findings. Possible or unconverged results stay labeled. The first 100 findings can seek the preview; totals include every finding. Along-route intervals do not guarantee physical clearance, especially at turns, self-crossings, open-end holds or connectors. Problems retains the production-wide analysis workflow after Apply.

A cancellable background worker checks the current/proposed move before Apply. It has a 20-second work budget and a separate 30-second watchdog. Retry after a failure, simplify the route/schedule or use fewer members. Cancel terminates unfinished work. Changed production, selected set/performers, playback or editing mode blocks stale acceptance. Save cannot commit a pending group draft. Apply creates one named undo transaction and preserves endpoint pictures, cast metadata, base paths and unrelated moves; Undo/Redo restores it atomically. Unchanged reviews create no undo entry.

**Copy reviewed group to another move** creates a new group only on that destination. With **Fit copied group counts**, entry/exit counts, cue clocks and delays scale by the duration ratio and speed scales inversely; phase, interval, route and member identities stay fixed. Without fitting, configured speed/delays stay fixed and the destination's route window is validated. Conflicting groups, endpoints, capacity or numeric/work budgets reject the copy. Source drafts remain uncommitted. Changing a move's counts through set properties or Move operations uses the same scaling, including dormant individual intervals.

**Move operations → Reverse** requires every member of each touched group. It swaps the selected endpoint positions, reverses the full program including connectors, stops and negative leader history, and reverses follow/counter-march member order so the previous last member becomes leader. Conveyor member order/offsets stay fixed. Dormant paths reverse as well. Adjacent endpoint effects and live constraints are still validated. Partial group reversal, grouped interval copy/paste, and splitting/rewiring a grouped move are unsupported and fail without partial edits. Explicitly remove groups before those operations. Copy/resize/reverse must still fit numeric and work budgets.

## Persistence and limits

Group programs use [format v12](../format/FORMAT.md#v12-group-route-movement): shared route reference, mode, ordered IDs, phase, speed, interval/offset direction, delay, connector counts, endpoint policy and cue clocks. The codec stores no generated path expansion or raw captured stroke. Older wire versions reject group fields; older apps reject v12 rather than discard motion. Projects without groups keep their least required version. Reopening restores the exact program and member order.

The route distance lookup retains #75's 0.001-yard whole-route chord/control-polygon length-gap budget and 0.0005-yard parameterization-deviation budget. Independent integration fixtures cover its ≤0.002-yard distance-position agreement. For consumers, each traversed lookup cell becomes an exact cropped line/cubic with count timing; playback and detailed polynomial analysis use those same pieces. Generated paths are runtime data, with binary-search sampling; they never become separately editable serialized approximations.

Generated distance cuts within floating-point roundoff (`16 × Number.EPSILON × max(routeLength, |startDistance|, |endDistance|)`) are coalesced. This prevents numerical slivers at exact lap and segment seams from creating false peak-stride findings. Authored cue/connector times and each travel leg's endpoints remain exact; this does not relax the stride or spacing rules.

Limits: 32 groups/move, 32 direction/stop cues/group, 2–500 disjoint members/group, speed 0.001–100 yd/count, delay 0–4,096 counts, phase ±1,000,000 yd, interval 0.01–1,000 yd, and the existing finite field/move/file limits. A single closed-route travel leg may cover at most 16 route lengths. Generated work is capped at 4,096 intervals/member, 100,000/move and 200,000/project. Unrepresentable tiny timings or excessive work reject explicitly. These limits also apply when reopening, copying or reversing; they never authorize a clipped/partial result.
