# Curved and independently timed movement checks

Issue [#76](https://github.com/IanSzymanski/squidware/issues/76) extends the background spacing/stride analyzer to cubic paths, performer holds, delayed departures, intermediate arrivals, gaps and mixed straight/curved schedules. Open **Show-wide problems** from the timeline or Inspector. Existing section rules and explicitly declared marching directions apply. A pair uses its stricter spacing limit. Directions are never inferred from the curve or field axis.

Spacing means minimum center-to-center distance over a move, in yards. Stride means maximum instantaneous distance per **written count**, including one-sided travel velocities at interval boundaries. It uses each travel's own duration/easing, so a short fast submove cannot hide inside a long average. Gaps/holds have zero velocity. Musical fermatas freeze the shared count; written-count analysis does not treat their extra seconds as extra travel counts. These checks do not model body/prop extents or authored facing.

## Reading a finding

Detailed findings expose conservative lower/upper bounds on the complete move's minimum spacing or maximum stride. The numerical target is an upper–lower gap of **0.001 yd** for spacing or **0.001 yd/count** for stride. Displayed quantity bounds round outwards so rounding does not exclude the true quantity; the displayed gap can therefore exceed the internal target. Coordinates/peak counts retain full numeric precision internally.

- **Confirmed violation:** a sampled distance is provably below the spacing limit, or a sampled velocity is provably above the stride limit, after arithmetic padding. The reported count identifies that witness. A count range around it follows a conservative velocity/acceleration bound and identifies a known offending window within that time piece. It does not enumerate every encounter in the move.
- **Possible violation:** the conservative bounds straddle the limit, without a violating witness. This includes numerical tangencies and extremely close boundary cases. The count range covers unresolved candidate intervals. Review it; a checked move can still contain possible findings.
- **Bounds remain unresolved:** the work/depth/representable-time budget prevented the target gap. The retained interval is still conservative; a violating witness can still confirm the finding. Uncertain findings remain warnings, never a silently clear result.

Danger requires a proven distance below half the pair's limit or a proven peak stride above 4/3 of the performer's limit. Other confirmed breaches and all possible breaches are warnings. A clean detailed result requires a spacing lower bound at/above the rule and a stride upper bound at/below it. Ordinary moves with only whole-move lines retain their prior exact formulas, threshold epsilon, text and ordering.

Selecting a finding pauses and seeks to its exact show count, explicitly selects its performers, highlights their full vector paths and samples their rings with the same movement evaluator as playback/3D. This also works during holds and fractional submoves. Undo/redo, save/reopen and path operations retain the geometry used by the analyzer. The Problems panel shows rule context, certainty, bounds and review ranges in every supported theme and with enlarged text.

## Conservative time-curve policy

Every track is compiled into continuous time pieces. Travel begins at the preceding arrival; final travel ends at the next picture. Explicit holds, implicit gaps and leading/trailing holds become constant pieces. All times are local written counts. Pair pieces split at the union of both performers' time boundaries, so asynchronous arrivals are compared at the same count.

A linear-timed line is degree one and a linear-timed cubic is degree three in normalized piece time. Smoothstep easing is a cubic Bernstein polynomial with controls `[0, 0, 1, 1]`. Composition gives a degree-three eased line or degree-nine eased cubic. Positive Bernstein product weights construct that representation without fitting to a sampling grid. Composition uses coordinates relative to the travel origin, and velocity controls are derived before the field origin is added back, avoiding false peaks from cancellation in tiny near-stationary windows. De Casteljau subdivision/cropping and degree elevation align the two performers, then coefficient subtraction gives their relative-position curve.

For relative position `R(t)`, its control-point box contains the whole curve. Distance from the origin to that box is a lower bound on `min |R(t)|`; distances at actual sampled times give upper bounds. For velocity `V(t)`, the derivative is another Bernstein curve, scaled by the piece's count duration. Convexity gives `max |V(t)| ≤ max |velocity controls|`; sampled velocities give lower bounds. Thus an unsampled crossing can lower the bound even when every nominal count sample looks clear.

A priority queue refines the most restrictive bound until the target gap is met. A near-zero distance witness can immediately use the universal zero lower bound, including unvisited time pieces. Threshold-straddling cases also refine time-varying candidate windows toward **1/1024 count**, within the same budget. Constant threshold quantities can remain uncertain throughout their interval. Confirmed windows use the witness margin divided by twice a conservative derivative-norm bound, clipped to its time piece. Possible windows use the envelope of remaining candidate pieces; disjoint possible encounters can produce a broad range.

Each search allows **2,048 subdivisions** and **depth 32**. A midpoint that cannot be represented ends refinement. Samples and curve splits use the actual represented local count, rather than evaluating an ideal midpoint and reporting a different rounded time. Confirmed ranges round inward and possible ranges outward when converted to show counts. If no adjacent representable show count lies within a confirmed witness window, the move is reported unavailable rather than navigating to a false witness. Control calculations add a guard of `1e-8 × max(1, largest control norm)` to quantity bounds; swept field boxes are padded similarly. Very short nonstationary intervals can make velocity or its padded bound unrepresentable. That reports the move as unavailable/not checked instead of emitting partial flags or false clearance. Numeric overflow is not treated as convergence. These are conservative real-geometry bounds with floating-point guards, rather than a claim of machine-verified interval arithmetic.

## Worker ownership and invalidation

Production geometry checks run in the worker, including curve compilation, broad-phase filtering, adaptive refinement and cache comparisons. A synchronous reference API exists for tests/tooling; gesture handlers do not call it. A separate preview worker retains the existing leading/trailing throttle. Detailed jobs yield at startup and at approximately 8 ms/4,096 cooperative steps, with cancellation checks before cache publication. Runtime/GC pauses can exceed that target; it is not a hard frame-time guarantee. Existing worker timeouts/errors remain visible.

The stats field `exactPairs` retains its historical name; for detailed moves it counts narrow-phase bounded pair checks, not exact polynomial solutions. Four move entries are retained. Per-track geometry, interval IDs/times, endpoints and resolved rules determine reuse; changed detailed tracks invalidate their stride and every touching pair, including former collisions outside new bounds. Global timing/rule/model changes invalidate the compatible move result. Cache inputs/compiled geometry are isolated from caller mutation. Editing a move clears its old findings synchronously; monotonic worker revisions, project epochs and show signatures reject stale replies, including during drag, undo and same-ID project replacement.

The independent test oracle evaluates power-basis cubics and derivatives and uses a dense bracketing grid plus golden-section refinement. Tests cover curved/chord-missed crossings, fast between-count windows, tangent/near-miss cases, asynchronous lines/holds, loops, per-direction rules and maximum-size schedules. The synchronous API shares the conservative numerical policy but bypasses worker caches and broad-phase pruning, so both layers have matching public findings.

## Output and compatibility

No project format changes: analysis metadata is transient. Existing v1–v6 migrations and sparse implicit lines remain compatible. Curved/timed coordinate sheets retain total distance and whole-move average step size; their single peak-step field stays blank, because a bounded peak is not one exact scalar. Problems exposes the peak-stride interval. Field path handles and detailed split/rewire operations retain their separate scope.

See [software review evidence](evidence/detailed-analysis/README.md) for source/native/desktop results, independent oracle coverage, rendered review, compute observations and actual worker responsiveness. The benchmark's coincident 32-interval case exercises every pair and uses the near-zero shortcut; it is not an upper bound for arbitrary numerical adversaries or physical hardware.
